Our Evaluation Process

We follow a strict, four-step pipeline to ensure every model is judged fairly and accurately.

1

Strict Dataset Curation

We don't use simple sentences. We created a specialized test set of 40 passages designed to challenge the limits of AI speech.

Tashkeel (Diacritic) Density: We use texts with high concentrations of vowels to see if the AI ignores them.

Nastaliq Complexity: We test complex Urdu word-joins to ensure the AI doesn't "collapse" the pronunciation.

Rapid Language Switches: We test how the AI handles switching between Urdu and Arabic every few words.

2

Sound Alignment

We compare the generated audio against the written text. We don't just check if the word sounds right; we check if the specific vowel marks (Tashkeel) are actually heard.

If an AI sounds "smooth" but skips a vowel that changes the meaning of a word, it is flagged as a Tashkeel Dropout.

3

Fluidity Analysis

We analyze the "jump" in sound when the speaker moves from Urdu to Arabic.

If there is a robotic glitch or a sudden shift in tone at the boundary, the model fails our Spectral Continuity test, as this destroys the professional quality of the speech.

4

Final Scoring (LFS)

We combine all these tests into one final score called the Linguistic Fidelity Score (LFS).

LFS = (Vowel Accuracy) + (Switching Fluidity) + (Overall Naturalness)

Models must score above 85% to be considered professionally stable.

Understanding the Science

Why standard AI metrics are not enough for Urdu and Arabic.

The "Vowel-Skipping" Problem

In Arabic, a single missing vowel mark (Tashkeel) can completely change the meaning of a sentence. Standard AI checks only if the letters are correct. Our system checks if the sound of the vowel is actually there.

Nastaliq Script Collapse

Urdu's Nastaliq style is visually complex. Many AI models simplify these shapes, causing the speech to sound robotic or unnatural. We use spectral analysis to ensure the speech remains fluid and professional.
